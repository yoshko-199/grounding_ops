# Proposal: judge a rounded count at the precision it was stated in

**Status: draft for review. Nothing here is decided, and no code changes
until it is.** It proposes an amendment to §9.2 of the
[specification](../claim-verification-engine.v0.5.md#92-tolerance-bands--a-fixed-rule-from-published-precision)
and to [AC-13](../acceptance-criteria.md#ac-13--tolerance-determinism). The
spec stays at v0.5 until a version bump adopts it. It follows from the scale
words that pack interface v1.8 introduced, and is listed as R10 in the
[requirements for the next version](v0.6-requirements.md#r10-rounded-counts).

---

## The problem

§9.2 gives discrete counts an exact match: "`u = 1`, tolerance zero". That
rule is right for what it was written for. A claim of `61` seats against a
certified `60` is wrong, and no tolerance should soften it.

It is wrong for a count the claimant stated at a coarser precision. On the
fixture, the latest registered job-seekers figure is `28,700`, and the measure
is declared `discrete`. Since interface v1.8 the engine reads "29 thousand"
as a figure stated to the nearest thousand. The rule still demands that the
published count equal `29,000` exactly, so the claim comes back
**Contradicted**, and the claim-level verdict becomes *False*.

That is a confident, sourced, wrong answer about a statement the record
supports. The claimant said "29 thousand"; the count, rounded to the
thousand, is `29,000`. §9.2's own Band A already accepts exactly this for a
continuous measure: "four percent" against a published `4.2` is correct "at
the claim's own stated precision". A count is the one kind of figure that
never gets that reading.

A second, smaller gap came to light in drafting this. §9.2 says a claim is
checked by rounding, but not which way a published figure exactly halfway
between two values rounds. The code rounds half to even, Python's default.
So against a published `28,500`, "28 thousand" matches and "29 thousand"
does not. The same holds for a continuous measure whenever the claim is
stated more coarsely than the series. Against a published `4.5` on a series
with precision `0.1`, "4" is Verified and "5" is **Contradicted**: `Δ` is
outside both allowances, so the rounding test alone decides. Both rounding
conventions are in everyday use. The engine has chosen one for the claimant,
and §9.2 does not let anything be chosen per claim.

## What must not change

- **A count written out in full is exact.** `28,701` against `28,700` is
  Contradicted, as today.
- **No allowance for counts.** No `Δ`, no relative band, no Band B slack. A
  count is right at the precision stated or it is wrong.
- **The precision comes from the claim's own text.** It is read, never
  chosen, and it is not configurable ([AC-3](../acceptance-criteria.md#ac-3--non-configurability)).
- **The binary overrides stay binary.** Superlatives and directions are
  untouched.

## The proposed rule

**Stated precision.** Let `s` be the unit of the last digit the claimant
wrote. If a scale word the pack's lexicon declares follows the number
(interface v1.8), multiply by that scale.

| Claim as written | `s` |
|---|---|
| `28,700` | `1` |
| `29,000` | `1` |
| `29 thousand` | `1,000` |
| `29k` | `1,000` |
| `28.7 thousand` | `100` |
| `1.2 million` | `100,000` |

Trailing zeros written out in full are digits, not rounding: `29,000` has
`s = 1`. See [the alternatives](#read-trailing-zeros-as-rounding) for why.

**Discrete counts** then resolve as follows:

| Condition | Element status |
|---|---|
| The claim equals the published count | **Verified** (Band A) |
| `s > 1`, and the published count rounds to the claim figure at `s` | **Verified**, tagged `rounded` |
| Otherwise | **Contradicted** |

When `s = 1` the middle row cannot apply, so a count written in full keeps
exact-match semantics unchanged.

A match at `s > 1` is tagged `rounded` even though Band A carries no tag for
a continuous figure. For a continuous measure, every published figure is
itself rounded to its precision, so a rounded claim is the normal case. For a
count, exactness is the norm, and a count that is right only at a stated
precision is worth the reader seeing. It surfaces as it does for Band B:
"Verified to within rounding, not exactly".

**Exact halves.** A published figure exactly halfway between two values at
the claim's precision rounds to either of them. This applies to the rounding
condition of Band A for every measure, not only to counts. Against `28,500`,
"28 thousand" and "29 thousand" both verify; against `4.5`, "4" and "5" both
do. Each is a correct rounding under a convention in common use, and the
engine has no basis for preferring one.

## Proposed text

In §9.2, the Band A condition becomes:

> **A** — The claim figure rounds to the published figure at the claim's own
> stated precision, **or** `Δ ≤ max(u/2, 0.5% relative)`. A published figure
> exactly halfway between two values at that precision rounds to either.

The discrete-count row of the binary overrides becomes:

> | Discrete counts (seats, votes, licences, recorded incidents) | Exact match at the precision the claim states. Let `s` be the unit of the last digit written, times any scale word the pack's lexicon declares after it. A claim equal to the published count is **Verified**. A claim with `s > 1` that the published count rounds to at `s` is **Verified**, tagged `rounded`. Anything else is **Contradicted**. There is no relative tolerance, and a count written in full (`s = 1`) must match exactly. Declared per measure via `discrete` in the pack. |

A paragraph is added after the table:

> **Stated precision is read, not inferred.** `s` comes from how the claim is
> written: its last written digit and a declared scale word. Trailing zeros
> are digits: a count written in full is a claim of exactness, and the engine
> does not guess that the claimant meant less. Words of approximation
> ("about", "around", "roughly") are not read as precision.

## Proposed AC-13 text

The second paragraph of the test becomes:

> Assert the binary overrides bypass the numeric bands: a superlative element
> with a near-miss series and a direction element with the wrong sign
> resolve to **Contradicted**, not Verified. For discrete counts, assert that
> a count written in full and off by one resolves to **Contradicted**; that a
> count stated with a scale word is **Verified**, tagged `rounded`, exactly
> when the published count rounds to it at the stated precision; that the
> same count off by one step of that precision is **Contradicted**; and that
> a count written in full with trailing zeros is held to exact match.

A new paragraph is added:

> Assert that a published figure exactly halfway between two values at the
> claim's precision verifies a claim of either neighbour, for a continuous
> measure and for a discrete count.

"**Fails if:**" gains: *or if a count's stated precision is taken from
anything but the claim's text.*

## Worked examples

On the fixture's registered job-seekers series: latest `28,700`, discrete.
The last two rows use a hypothetical published `28,500` to show the half
rule.

| Claim | `s` | Today | Proposed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `28,700` | `1` | Verified | Verified |
| `28,701` | `1` | Contradicted | Contradicted |
| `29,000` | `1` | Contradicted | Contradicted |
| `28.7 thousand` | `100` | Verified | Verified (equal, so no tag) |
| `29 thousand` | `1,000` | Contradicted | **Verified, tagged `rounded`** |
| `28 thousand` | `1,000` | Contradicted | Contradicted |
| `30 thousand` | `1,000` | Contradicted | Contradicted |
| `28 thousand` against `28,500` | `1,000` | Contradicted | **Verified, tagged `rounded`** |
| `29 thousand` against `28,500` | `1,000` | Contradicted | **Verified, tagged `rounded`** |

For a continuous measure the only change is at exact halves, and it matters
only where the `Δ` allowances do not already reach. On a series with
precision `0.1`, "4" against a published `4.5` is Verified today and "5" is
Contradicted; under the proposal, both are Verified. "4.3" against `4.25`
is unaffected: its `Δ` is within Band A's allowance either way.

## Alternatives considered

### Read trailing zeros as rounding

Treat `29,000` as stated to the thousand. **Not recommended.** The same
string is an exact claim as often as a rounded one. A vote tally of `61,000`
may be exact, and reading it as "about sixty-one thousand" would verify it
against `61,412`. The rule would then turn on how many zeros a figure
happens to end in, not on anything the claimant chose. A claimant who means
"about" has "29 thousand", "29k" and "about 29,000" available, and the
first two are read.

### A relative tolerance for large counts

Accept counts within some small percentage. **Rejected.** The percentage
would be a number someone chose. The discrete rule exists precisely so that
no tolerance is chosen for counts.

### Leave rounded counts unverified

Mark a count stated with a scale word *unverified* rather than judge it.
**Rejected.** The record does settle it: the published count either rounds
to the claim or it does not. Declining to answer would be Insufficient Data
standing in for a check the engine can make.

## What it touches

- **AC-13:** rewritten as above.
- **AC-3:** `s` is derived from the claim's text and the pack's declared
  scale words, so nothing becomes configurable.
- **AC-7:** nothing new is rendered. The claim is quoted as written, and the
  published count is cited as published. The rounded figure the comparison
  uses is never output, as with scaled figures since interface v1.8.
- **Bottom line:** no change needed. A count Verified with the `rounded` tag
  already reads "right to within rounding, not exactly".

## Implementation sketch, once accepted

- In `engine/verification/tolerance.py`, the discrete branch of `band_for`
  becomes:
  - equal → Band A;
  - otherwise, if the claim's exponent is positive and `_rounds_to` holds →
    Band B, `rounded=True`;
  - otherwise → Band C.

  The claim's exponent already encodes `s`. A scaled value keeps the
  precision it was stated at (`Decimal("29").scaleb(3)` has exponent `3`),
  and a count written in full has exponent `0`, so `s = 1` needs no special
  case.
- `_rounds_to` accepts a match under either `ROUND_HALF_UP` or
  `ROUND_HALF_DOWN`, instead of the context's `ROUND_HALF_EVEN`.
- Tests: the AC-13 assertions above, one per row of the worked examples, and
  a negative check that restoring exact match for scaled counts, or
  half-even rounding, fails them.
- Docs: `docs/reference/glossary.md` (tolerance band) and
  `docs/reference/pack-schema.md` (`discrete`).

## Open questions

1. **Words of approximation.** Should a pack be able to declare words such
   as "about", "around" and "roughly", read as widening `s` to the last
   non-zero digit? That would let "about 29,000" be judged as "29 thousand"
   is. It needs care: "nearly" and "over" are bounds, not approximations,
   and belong to comparative derivation, not tolerance.
2. **A floor on stated precision.** `0.03 million` has `s = 10,000` and
   would verify against `28,700`. That is what the claimant asserted, and the
   `rounded` tag and the cited count show the reader the exact figure. Any
   floor, such as a minimum number of significant digits, would be a number
   someone chose. The recommendation is no floor.
3. **Scope of the half rule.** It is proposed for every measure, because the
   defect is in the shared rounding test. If adopting it for counts only is
   preferred, the continuous case should still state its convention in
   §9.2, since the code currently supplies one silently.
